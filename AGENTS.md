# yapa

How yapa is built and how to work on it. Goals and how they are measured are in GOALS.md; fium rules in ~/Documents/fium/RULES.md.

## Target
iPhone SE (1st gen, A9, arm64, not arm64e), iOS 15.8, jailbroken with Dopamine (rootless, semi-untethered).

- **Daemons** are Go binaries built by `./build-ios` into `build/ios` (Go links ios/arm64 only through cgo, so it uses Xcode's iPhoneOS SDK), signed with `ldid`, and run as launchd daemons from `/var/jb/usr/libexec/yapa/`. `yata` watches the services listed in `/var/jb/etc/yapa/services.json` (read again whenever it changes) and answers `/health` on port 8077, to the home network only. Its `/parts` lists the core and every part, on or off and up or down, with disk free and log sizes; only the phone itself (the pad) may POST `/parts/<name>/on`, `off` or `restart`, which writes the part's down file and runs the setup `./ya` leaves on the phone (`/var/jb/etc/yapa/setup`, with `Caddyfile.core` and `domain`), so a part turned on or off there is regenerated exactly as `./ya` would. A part that's off keeps its binary on the phone, so it can come back without the Mac, and `./ya` takes the phone's down files before every sync. It also cuts the logs in `/var/jb/var/log/yapa` hourly: 5 MB each, 50 MB in all.
- **`./ya install root@<ip>`** sends the core (yata, Caddy, cloudflared, with their plists in `launchd/`) and every part over one ssh connection, unloads and reloads them. It needs root SSH by key. On rootless Dopamine root's home is `/var/jb/var/root`, so the key goes in `/var/jb/var/root/.ssh/authorized_keys`, not `/var/root/.ssh`. The plist needs no rootless changes: launchd loads it from `/var/jb/Library/LaunchDaemons/` (owned root:wheel) and runs the ldid-signed binary as is.
- **Parts** are everything else on the phone: `~/.config/yapa/parts/<name>/part` holds `key=value` lines (`name`, `kind` site or service, `run`, `port`, `check` in yata's format, `address`, `needs`, `mem`), and a `down` file beside it means installed but off. `./ya add site|service`, `./ya on|off` and `./ya parts` manage them. Each sync regenerates the phone's service plists, `services.json` and Caddyfile (from `phone/Caddyfile`) from the core and the parts that are on, so a part is watched by yata and its log, in `/var/jb/var/log/yapa`, is tidied with no extra step. `needs=` lists parts that must be on first. Commands reach the phone `./ya install` last used (`~/.config/yapa/host`); an address is a Caddy block plus a DNS name made with the Mac's `cloudflared tunnel route dns`, never the apex, www or the main site.
- **jeta**, the pad (shown on the phone as **miga**: its ants are crumbs, not faces), is a native app (Swift, UIKit, iOS 15), installed with TrollStore. It is a Claude Hardware Buddy: a CoreBluetooth peripheral on the Nordic UART Service, speaking newline-delimited JSON as specified in https://github.com/anthropics/claude-desktop-buddy/blob/main/REFERENCE.md. It advertises a name starting with `Claude`, requires encryption on its characteristics, and echoes `prompt.id` exactly in `{"cmd":"permission","id":…,"decision":"once"|"deny"}`. iOS advertises the name only while the app is in the foreground, so jeta stays in front.
- The protocol core (parsing, state, decisions) is a Swift package with no UIKit or CoreBluetooth imports, so `bench/jeta-buddy` runs it on the Mac.
- `jeta/` holds JetaKit (the face and ant models, no UIKit), BuddyKit (the Hardware Buddy protocol, no UIKit or CoreBluetooth) and `App/` (UIKit: the view that draws the ants, and the BLE link). `jeta/build-app sim` builds `build/jeta/sim/jeta.app` for the Simulator; `jeta/build-app device` builds `build/jeta/jeta.ipa` for TrollStore, and `jeta/install` puts it on the phone over SSH, restarts bluetoothd so the Claude desktop app reconnects by itself, and waits for heartbeats. The app logs the link to `Documents/buddy.log` on the phone. There is no Xcode project: swiftc compiles both straight from source. iOS 27 Simulators refuse apps without a scene delegate, so the app uses one.
- Claude's desktop app lists a device only if macOS's name for it starts with `Claude`, and for a phone that's the phone's own name, so the SE is named `Claude-yapa`. Its status ack must carry every `bat`, `sys` and `stats` field, and it sends `prompt` only for short, simple requests; other waiting prompts arrive as `waiting` alone.
- The screen shows one white ant per session (JetaKit's `Colony`, designed in `jeta/proto/ants`), each acting out its session's mood; the faces (`Face`, two small white ovals drawn from `Stroke.outline()`) are still the mood models behind them (blinks, moods, dozing off) but are never drawn. An ant has a real ant's shape: head (with a pair of mandibles), thorax, a small waist, an abdomen that trails like a rope, and six long, nearly straight legs from the thorax. Its legs follow RujiK's procedural salamanders: each foot stays planted until its step spot (half a stride ahead) is a stride away, then steps there, one tripod at a time, and the step twists the body; the head bobs with the stride. Each session has its own spot (one in the middle, more in two columns of rows, in the order they started; a session another started stands on a ring round that one, below first), and its ant stays within about 30 pt of it, walking around other ants, never over them, so where an ant is tells you whose it is. An ant is in one of four situations (the spec is `jeta/proto/animations.md`). **Needs you** (a permission, a question, or a failed turn, whose error is its text): a short freeze and pop, then the one forward runs to the middle, grows and steps closer to you and back (as if toward the camera, a little bigger, legs stepping, never a throb) every 3 s for a minute and every 15 s after, while the others hold still; a second one does it at its own spot at half the rate; allowed, it squashes and hurries home, denied, it droops and walks home slowly. **Busy** (thinking or running tools alike): it walks slowly about its spot and never stops (thinking, with no tool running, its antennae drum and its head shakes as it goes), Reynolds' wander on a leash: its heading drifts smoothly and it's pulled back harder the farther it gets past half its leash (24–32 pt, its own), each ant at its own speed, slower after five minutes; no errand per hook event. `bench/jeta-ants` measures burn-in as the share of an hour the most-covered pixel spends under an ant. **Your turn** (done, then resting): done turns once on the spot with its antennae up, then it stands; while a question or actions of its session wait, it faces you and walks toward you, as if toward the camera, stepping in place as it grows to 1.25 times (Colony's `asking`), then rears up to look at you, drawn in perspective from above: head and thorax tip up toward the lens about the waist (nearer the middle and bigger, the head covering the thorax), the abdomen stays on the ground behind, the front legs come up and the others brace wide, and the antennae and mandibles show in front of the head; it walks back and settles once nothing waits; and once its session has been quiet a while (the face model dozing, after about 95 s) it curls up, antennae folded, still creeping, but never while a question or actions of it wait, crumbs sit beside it, or it's stale. `jeta/RULES.md` lists every rule the pad keeps, each with what checks it. **Gone**: it walks off the nearest edge. Ants are always full white. Held to talk, only the held ant reacts: it steps about 40 pt out from under the fingertip, keeps its size, faces the finger, nods on release and walks back. While a session's text is open the others walk off the nearest edge one after another (85 pt/s) and come back nearest first. Every still ant creeps along a slow drift (0.35–0.95 pt/s) so nothing burns in. Text comes out of an ant only when its session needs you; a reply opens on a tap, or by itself when it ends on a question. A busy ant drops a crumb each minute of its turn into one tight pile 25 pt beside its spot, on the side clearest of other ants and edges (ants walk around piles, never over them), and picks them up once you've seen the finished turn (its reply opened on the pad, a new prompt, or the Claude app showing the session since, from che's `/sessions` `focused`); touching an ant shows its name with what it's on and for how long, and the session list (swipe down) says the same for each. 60 fps while one walks or acts, 30 otherwise. White only. `bench/jeta-ants` measures it.
- The manager session's `jeta:` lines arrive as turn events from every session; `NoticeBoard` reads them and `Screen` decides what shows (question, detail, session list) and what each gesture does. On the bare face a tap pokes it awake and a swipe down opens the session list; with text up a tap opens the detail, a tap on the detail puts a question away into its ant (a tap on that ant brings it back; other taps leave it away), and a swipe sideways moves between sessions (the text follows the finger 1:1 while it drags and springs back if let go short; a drag that began as a hold drops it, unsent); reading a reply, a drag up or down follows the finger the same way and turns the page (the reply is one column: the pages before and after wait just past the band its page shows in, fading at its edges, so the next comes up under the finger, and only the reply's lines move, not its title or hint); past the first or last page, or sideways with nowhere to go, the text goes on iOS's rubber band (`TextMotion.rubber`, UIScrollView's `(1 − 1/(0.55·x/d + 1))·d`) and springs back; what a request would do is never cut: its detail pages the same way, to its last word, with its buttons on every page; a tap on an option is that option even in the 2 s a tap would stop a pending Enter. Holding anywhere is talking (Talk, through che); while a permission request is up, holding denies it and the words are the reason (che's `/talk?dry`). A request offers Allow, This session (when it comes with `permission_suggestions`, added with `destination: session`), Deny and Stop (deny with `interrupt`). `FaceView.take(_:)` is where the Buddy link feeds events; in the Simulator, launch with `-jeta "jeta: …"` to try a screen. Text moves like the face: `Screen.place` lays the lines out and `TextMotion` gives each one springs, keyed by its words so a line on both screens glides; lines come out of the face after it gets out of their way, a swipe carries the page its way, a chosen option pops and stays lit, and Talk's notes take the hint's place. The SE's IPS LCD keeps a ghost of anything left still for hours, so all text creeps together along `Screen.drift` (slow waves plus a 3 pt circle, at most 0.23 pt/s), text left up three minutes untouched goes back into its ant (a question folds until a new one comes or a tap brings it back; only a permission request stays), and once nobody has touched the pad for three minutes and nothing is happening (no session working or needing you) the backlight fades to 0.08, back on a touch or anything that needs you. `bench/jeta-text` counts jumps, text over the face, and how long text stays still or up untouched. `bench/jeta-flows` runs every flow the pad offers end to end in a booted Simulator: launched with `-drive <port>` the pad takes taps, swipes, holds and twists over HTTP through the same code the gestures reach and reports its screen (`App/Drive.swift`, Simulator builds only), and `-che host:port` and `-yata <url>` point it at the bench's stand-ins; a new gesture or screen gets a flow there. `jeta/pictures` records the README's animated SVGs from the same Simulator pad: `/record` takes every shape and text layer as vectors on the display's tick.
- Two fingers landing open the dial, for what has no other way in (sessions are answered where they are, by tapping, swiping and holding). JetaKit's `Chord` reads it (after Krenn's car UI and Clak Remote's dials): twisting turns it a step per π/8, clockwise up; spreading, pinching or sliding never does; one finger never acts, three lock as nothing, a finger landing late voids it, and letting go of two is a release. JetaKit's `Dial` is what it turns through, as words like the rest of the screen: the word it's on in the middle, big and bold, what it is under it, its neighbours smaller and dimmer, and every ant walks off while it's open. Its first level is new session (the folders sessions ran in, which `Desk` keeps from the hooks' `cwd`; letting go on one has che open `claude://code/new?folder=` in the Claude app, the link its own Finder service uses), server (every part from yata's `/parts`, up or down and on or off, each with turn on or off and restart) and tunnel and disk; a level below starts with back, a tap or 12 s untouched puts it away. `App/Menu.swift` builds it and runs its acts. `bench/jeta-chord` replays seeded twists through `Chord`.
- The Hardware Buddy link carries no text from device to desktop, so push-to-talk has its own path. `jeta/App/Talk.swift`: hold the face (≥150 ms) to record 16 kHz mono WAV while the face shows `.listening`, release (back to the mood before) to POST it to `che` on the Mac, port 8078, at the address the hooks come from (Bonjour, `_che._tcp`, can hand back a link-local address che refuses, so it is used only before any hook has come); a tap in the next 2 s POSTs `/cancel`. `Talk.say(_:to:)` POSTs text (an option picked on the screen) to `/say`, which che types the same way, Enter and `/cancel` included; each pick carries `?key=`, kept while no answer comes, so one picked again after the Mac didn't answer is taken once and answered with what became of the first. Both carry `?session=` with the Claude Code session id from the hooks: the face held (or the only one), and for an option the session whose `jeta:` line asked. che finds that session's `local_…` id in the Claude app's `claude-code-sessions` files, opens it with `claude://code/continue?session=…` (the link raises the app, so che gives the front back to the app you had there once the text is sent), and types into the app's process only once the app's `main.log` says `setFocusedSession` for it. A session the app doesn't have is looked up by its exact id in the records Claude Code keeps for each running session (`~/.claude/sessions/<pid>.json`, trusted only while that pid's start time still matches), and its process's tty names the Terminal tab it runs in: che selects that tab over AppleScript, brings Terminal forward, and keys the text to Terminal's process only once that tab is the front window's; the Enter is pressed only if it still is, and arrival is read from the session's transcript (`~/.claude/projects/*/<id>.jsonl`, the prompt taken or queued while busy). A session in neither (another terminal app, or none), or one that didn't come forward in 3 s, leaves the words on the clipboard, never in another window. Claude Code's channels (`--channels`, a research preview) would need every session started with them, and its cross-session sockets deliver as a message from another agent, not your prompt, so neither is used.
- **Hooks over Wi-Fi.** `jeta/hooks on [host]` (JavaScript in osascript, nothing to install) puts hooks in `~/.claude/settings.json` beside the user's own that curl `http://<phone>:8079/hook` with a 1 s connect timeout; the phone is the address `./ya install` saved in `~/.config/yapa/host`, since iOS answers its `claude-yapa.local` name over mDNS only some of the time (a lookup can hang 10 s), and the name only when none is saved, so a phone that's off fails every hook fast. Each ends in `# jeta`, which is how `jeta/hooks off` finds exactly them; the first `on` keeps the file as `settings.json.before-yapa`. Status events run in the background (`async`); `PermissionRequest` waits up to 10 min. The terminal's own dialog shows at the same time and whichever answers first wins; an answer there doesn't end the hook, so the pad lets it go with no decision on the session's next event. jeta's `HookListener` (App/Hooks.swift) takes them from the home network only, BuddyKit's `Desk` keeps every session's one state, by precedence (needs you, failed, stale: working with nothing running and no news for 10 min, or an hour on one tool call or subagent, working, done, quiet; notifications aren't news), where a Stop counts as done only 5 s later and only once its subagents have stopped (kept by `agent_id` and `agent_type` from `SubagentStart`/`SubagentStop`, with each one's tool calls, which carry its `agent_id`), a `PostToolUseFailure` ends its step like `PostToolUse`, and one with `is_interrupt` (its own or a subagent's) ends the turn and stops its subagents, since no Stop or SubagentStop follows an interrupt (one that goes on in the background runs again on its next tool call), (named by folder until che's `GET /sessions?ids=` gives the Claude app's name: the short name a side session was started with, else its title cut to a few words, with the session that started or forked it, read from `claude-code-sessions`; asked when a new session reports and every 30 s) and turns it into the heartbeat the Claude app sends, fed to the same Buddy core; a tap's decision answers the held hook. While any session reports, Bluetooth input is ignored, so the manager's `jeta:` lines come from the hooks too: `Desk.turn` makes a turn event of a Bash `echo` (PreToolUse) and of the text a session stopped on (Stop's `last_assistant_message`). No answer, a timeout or the phone off gives the hook no decision: only a tap approves. `test/jeta-hooks-sim` runs the listener in a booted Simulator.
- `mac/` is the Mac side (SwiftPM, macOS 26+). `TalkKit` holds what tests can reach: WER, language pick, HTTP parsing, routing, the pending Enter, and `Transcriber` (Apple's on-device SpeechTranscriber, en_US and es_ES run in parallel, the more confident wins). `che` types the text with CGEvents into Claude or a terminal in front, else keys it into the Claude app behind without raising it, else leaves it on the clipboard; Enter follows after `CHE_ENTER` s (2). A session the pad names gets `CHE_WAIT` s (3) to come into focus. After the Enter, che waits up to 4 s for the app's log to say it sent the text to that session; if it doesn't, the text goes on the clipboard. A Terminal session's text that isn't in its transcript within 4 s stays pending (che keeps watching 2 min, since a busy session takes it later), and one whose tab was no longer in front for the Enter goes on the clipboard. che types one delivery at a time: from bringing its session forward until its Enter is in and the front given back (or the Enter cancelled), any other text goes on the clipboard, answered with no Enter to wait for, so it never joins the first as one prompt and the first stays trackable and stoppable. The pad asks `/sent?id=` once the Enter is in (`state`: sent, pending or copied) and says Sent, Pending (asking again), Copied, or Not confirmed when che doesn't say; text typed with no session named says Typed. `bench/che-live` runs a pick end to end through a real che into a scratch session in the Claude app. It accepts only this Mac and `CHE_FROM` hosts, IPv4 only, and like the pad's `/hook` refuses what a web page sends (a request with `Origin` or `Sec-Fetch-*`), since a page open on the Mac reaches 127.0.0.1 too. Nothing in the desktop app or `claude` accepts text from outside, short of `claude --channels` with a dev flag, so typing is the one path that reaches both. `mac/install` runs it as a LaunchAgent.
